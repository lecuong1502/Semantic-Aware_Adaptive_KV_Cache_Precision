\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

\textit{MicroInfer} keeps a generation session alive when another process on the
same consumer GPU takes video memory it did not expect to lose. It changes the
precision at which the key-value cache is held, page by page and while decoding
continues, and chooses which pages to degrade by how much attention the model
pays to them.

The solution has two layers. The \textbf{engine} runs the model over CUDA kernels
written for this work and holds the cache on \textit{pages}, the keys and values
of $P = 32$ consecutive positions of one layer, each at one of four
\textit{precision tiers}: FP16, INT8, INT4 or INT2. Pages come from the CUDA
virtual memory management interface, one address range per tier, so that memory
a smaller tier frees returns to the driver instead of staying with the engine.
The \textbf{control loop} decides, while the engine runs, which page is held at
which tier. It has four components (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}):
\begin{itemize}
\item a \textbf{VRAM pressure monitor}, which polls the driver's free memory on a
background thread and classifies it as GREEN, YELLOW or RED, with hysteresis;
\item an \textbf{attention importance scorer}, which keeps, for every page, an
exponentially weighted average of the attention mass on its positions;
\item a \textbf{precision controller}, which turns the pressure level, the
importance scores and the current tiers into a \textit{requantisation plan},
downgrading the least important pages under pressure and upgrading the most
important first once it passes; it contains no device code, so the policy can be
tested, and compared with a uniform one, in isolation;
\item a \textbf{paged mixed-precision cache manager}, which applies the plan by
requantising each page into a new one at its target tier and freeing the old.
\end{itemize}

A plan is applied only \textit{between} decoding steps, never inside a kernel
launch, so no kernel sees the cache half converted; attention reads each page at
its current tier, dequantising as it loads. Three properties hold throughout:
memory a downgrade frees reaches the driver, where both the process that needs it
and the measurement can see it; a tier belongs to a \textit{(layer, page)} pair,
not to a token position; and the recent context stays at FP16, both because the
page still being filled cannot be quantised --- a key channel's scale spans the
whole page --- and because a recency floor protects recent positions as a matter
of policy.

\subsection{The From-Scratch Boundary}
\label{sec:boundary}

\textit{MicroInfer} is built from scratch in the sense this thesis depends on:
every kernel that the research contribution touches is hand-written CUDA, and no
external inference engine --- vLLM, llama.cpp or TensorRT-LLM --- is used at any
point. The phrase stops at one place, the dense matrix multiplications, which
call NVIDIA's cuBLAS library. This subsection states that boundary explicitly, so
that no later result is read as implying the projections were hand-written.

\paragraph{Hand-written.} The following are CUDA kernels or host code written for
this engine:
\begin{itemize}
\item the token embedding lookup;
\item RMSNorm, over fp16 activations or over the fp32 residual stream;
\item rotary position embedding (RoPE), with its angles formed in fp64, and
tabulated once per position for the keys the cache completes;
\item the SwiGLU activation;
\item the broadcast of a projection's bias into its output rows, on which the
second precision choice below relies;
\item attention: causal scaled dot-product attention with an online softmax,
grouped-query attention, and the completion of keys cached without their
projection's bias --- over a contiguous cache, over pages, and over pages at a
quantised tier, dequantising each code as it is loaded;
\item the paged key-value cache: its page table, the stores that write it, and
the allocator beneath it, which uses the CUDA virtual memory management API so
that memory it frees returns to the driver;
\item quantisation and dequantisation of a page at INT8, INT4 and INT2;
\item greedy token selection over the logits.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{cuBLAS.} The dense projections --- the query, key, value and output
projections of attention, the gate, up and down projections of the MLP, and the
language-model head --- call \texttt{cublasGemmEx}, through a single wrapper.
cuBLAS is a basic linear algebra library, not an inference engine: it multiplies
the matrices it is given and knows nothing of models, caches or precision tiers.

\paragraph{Why the boundary is here.} Hand-writing the matrix multiplications as
well was considered and rejected. Every latency figure this thesis reports would
inherit the gap between a hand-written GEMM and cuBLAS, and a reader could no
longer separate the overhead of the adaptive precision policy from the cost of a
slow matrix multiplication --- the very separation the systems claim rests on.
When the decision was taken the gap was estimated at two to five times. It was
then measured, in the study described last in this subsection, on the RTX 4050
Laptop GPU used throughout, at the driver's own clocks and with the desktop's
processes sharing the GPU. On the models' own projections at 512 rows, a
shared-memory-tiled kernel ran 8--28 times slower than cuBLAS and a naive one
93--267 times slower. In single-token decoding the tiled kernel ran 5--21 times
slower; those timings flatter every implementation somewhat, since a repeated
launch finds a small projection's weights still in the 24\,MiB L2 cache, which a
whole model does not fit. The measurement strengthens the decision rather than
revising it.

\paragraph{Two precision choices.} The wrapper makes two choices on which the
per-kernel correctness figures reported for the projections depend --- a
relative error of at most four units in the last place of fp16 and a mean below
one, against a float64 reference.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{fp32 accumulation, with reduced-precision reduction disallowed.}
Operands are fp16 and the computation is fp32 (\texttt{CUBLAS\_COMPUTE\_32F}).
The handle is also set to
\texttt{CUBLAS\_MATH\_DISALLOW\_REDUCED\_PRECISION\_REDUCTION}: without it,
cuBLAS may reduce the partial sums of a split-K computation in the output's
precision, which is a silent fp16 accumulation inside a call that asked for fp32.
\item \textbf{The bias folded in, so the result is rounded once.} Where a
projection carries a bias, the output rows are first seeded with it and the
multiplication adds onto them. The sum is formed in fp32 inside cuBLAS and
rounded to fp16 once, rather than rounded after the multiplication and again
after a separate addition.
\end{enumerate}
The same principle, to round as late and as seldom as possible, applies to the
projections without a bias: the output and down projections add straight into
the fp32 residual stream, and the language-model head writes fp32 logits, so
neither is rounded to fp16 at all.

\paragraph{The GEMM study.} A naive GEMM kernel and a shared-memory-tiled one were
written and profiled against cuBLAS with Nsight Compute, as a study of where
their time goes. At 512 rows the naive kernel is bound by its load and store
queue, the tiled one by shared memory, and cuBLAS by the tensor cores, which
neither hand-written kernel uses. In decoding the projection becomes a
matrix-vector product: cuBLAS switches to a kernel without tensor cores that
approaches the memory bandwidth, and the hand-written kernels stall well short of
it. Both compute the projection correctly, to the same per-kernel bound
as the engine's. The study is a learning and benchmarking artifact and nothing
more: it is not part of the inference path, and the engine's build, its import
graph and its packaged form are each tested not to reach it.

\subsection{Contention Characterisation}
\label{sec:spikes}
The traces of RQ1 are read by one analysis, fixed before any trace was
recorded. Free memory is sampled at 50 Hz. Its \textit{baseline} is the rolling
median of the preceding 5 s, and a \textit{spike} is free memory at least
64 MiB below the baseline for at least 100 ms; 64 MiB is an order of magnitude
above the noise of an idle desktop on the machine used, a few 2 MiB granules.
The threshold, the window and the minimum duration are parameters, and the raw
traces are kept, so every figure can be recomputed at another threshold.

Four rules make the definition hold on a real trace.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The baseline holds during a spike, for one window at most.} A
median left to roll would sink into any spike longer than half its window and
end it there. From a spike's first sample the baseline is held; a spike still
below it after a whole window is a \textit{lasting drop}, reported as not
recovered, after which the new level becomes the baseline.
\item \textbf{A spike ends only when its deficit falls below half the
threshold.} Without this hysteresis, noise on a spike near the threshold would
split it into several.
\item \textbf{A gap in the trace ends a spike.} A spike cut by a gap, or by the
end of the trace, is \textit{censored}: its duration is a lower bound, and
nothing is interpolated across the gap.
\item \textbf{A measure stays within its own spike.} Where two spikes follow too
closely for a measure to be taken, it is reported as missing rather than
stretched across both.
\end{itemize}

For each spike four measures are taken from its \textit{deficit}, the held
baseline minus free memory, with crossing times interpolated between samples.
The \textit{amplitude} is the largest deficit. The \textit{peak} is the run of
samples at or above 90\% of the amplitude around it. The \textit{rise time} runs
from the last crossing of 10\% of the amplitude before the peak to the peak's
start; the \textit{recovery} runs from the peak's end to the next crossing of
10\%, its mirror. The \textit{duration} is the time the deficit spends at or
above the threshold.

Each spike is attributed to the process most responsible for it, from the
per-process memory sampled at 5 Hz: the process whose holding gained most
between the last sample before the rise began and the samples taken during the
peak, provided the gain is at least a quarter of the amplitude, reported with
its share. Otherwise the spike names no process and records why: no sample fell
within its peak, the driver did not report the holdings, or no process gained
enough. Spikes are reported per scripted action, every action included, and as
rates per hour of recorded time for passive sessions; the counts are repeated at
thresholds of 32 and 128 MiB. Distributions of duration and recovery are drawn
from recovered spikes only, since a censored duration is a lower bound.
